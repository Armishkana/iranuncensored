      Who will govern with whom: what each leader has said
      • Likud
        • Netanyahu has said a government must not rely on the Arab parties. Source, 27 October 2026
        • Netanyahu told Ben-Gvir and Smotrich after the vote: "We are together and we are staying together." Source, 28 October 2026
      • Yashar
        • Eisenkot has said he will not serve under Netanyahu, not even in a rotation agreement. Source, 21 October 2026
        • Eisenkot will form only a Zionist government: partners must recognise Israel as a Jewish state and call Hamas a terrorist group. Source, 28 October 2026
        • Eisenkot on Ra'am and the Joint List: "I do not see an Arab party that accepts my principles." Source, 24 September 2026
      • Together
        • Bennett has said he will not sit with Netanyahu in any government. Source, 12 October 2026
        • Bennett has said he will not rely on the Arab parties. Source, 15 July 2026
      • Joint List
        • The Joint List asks for no ministries and would consider backing a government against Netanyahu from outside. Source, 28 October 2026
      • The Democrats
        • The Democrats have ruled out joining a government with Netanyahu and Likud. Source, 1 October 2026
        • After the vote Golan urged a government of change "in partnership with Israel's Arab citizens". Source, 28 October 2026
        • Golan on Ben-Gvir's and Smotrich's parties: "With them I will not sit." Source, 8 November 2025
        • Golan on the ultra-Orthodox parties: "I do not disqualify the haredim, absolutely not." Source, 27 May 2026
      • Ra'am
        • Abbas wants Ra'am inside a government of change: "If we join a government of change, we will be able to see substantial changes." Source, 27 October 2026
        • Ra'am said it would recommend Eisenkot to Herzog. Source, 4 November 2026
      • Yisrael Beytenu
        • Liberman has said he will not sit with Netanyahu, with UTJ's Yitzhak Goldknopf, with Shas's Aryeh Deri or with the Joint List's Ahmad Tibi. Source, 29 October 2026
        • Liberman has said the government must not rely on Arab parties, either directly or indirectly. Source, 29 October 2026
      • Otzma Yehudit
        • Ben-Gvir: "I don't want Eisenkot to be prime minister." Source, 23 October 2026
      • United Torah Judaism
        • UTJ sources, unnamed, have floated backing an Eisenkot government from outside. It is a report, not a party decision. Source, 29 October 2026
        • UTJ said it would not recommend anyone for prime minister. Source, 4 November 2026
      • Shas
        • Deri has said of Shas: "We support Netanyahu, period." Source, 16 July 2026
      • Religious Zionism
        • Smotrich would not rule out a government with Eisenkot: "We don't boycott anyone." He added: "I go with my natural partners." Source, 22 October 2026

      What happens next

      1. 28 October 2026Herzog asks the parties to avoid another election and come to talks with an open mind. Source
      2. 28 October 2026Netanyahu opens talks with his allies, Smotrich and Ben-Gvir first. Source
      3. 4 November 2026Official results presented to Herzog. RZP and Shas recommend Netanyahu; Yisrael Beytenu recommends Eisenkot. Source
      4. 5 November 2026Herzog's talks with the parties end: 67 MKs recommend Eisenkot (Yashar, Together, The Democrats, Yisrael Beytenu, Ra'am, Joint List), 46 Netanyahu. UTJ recommends no one. Source
      5. 8 November 2026Herzog names who gets the first chance to form a government, after talking first with some party leaders. Source
      6. 10 November 2026The new Knesset is expected to be sworn in. Source
      7. 18 November 2026Last day to challenge the results. Source
      8. ThenAfter the results, Herzog meets every party and picks the leader most likely to form a government. That leader has 28 days, and 14 more if asked. If two leaders fail, the Knesset gets 21 days; then a new election. Source
